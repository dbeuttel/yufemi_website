# Yufemi Wellness: Outstanding Issues & Recommendations

_Last updated: November 18, 2026_

This is a plain-English to-do list for getting Yufemi's website and online store into great shape. No technical knowledge needed. Each item says **what's wrong**, **why it matters**, and **what to do**.

**How to read the priorities**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|

## Part 1: Decisions only the owner can make

These are blocking the new website. Nothing technical is needed; just answers.

### 1. 🟠👤 How will customers pay?
- **What's wrong:** The new website is a fast, simple "brochure" site. It can't run a shopping cart by itself. Right now its "Buy" buttons send people to the shop on the *old* website. If the domain is switched over before this is decided, **the Buy buttons will stop working**.
- **Options (pick one):**
  - **A. Keep the current store** (WooCommerce) at a separate address like `shop.yufemi.com`. Least change, but you keep paying for and maintaining the old WordPress setup.
  - **B. Move the store to Shopify** (about $5–39/month). Easiest to run long-term; has built-in subscriptions, reviews and shipping tools.
  - **C. Use Stripe "Payment Links."** A simple checkout page per product. Cheapest and simplest while you have 1–3 products; no cart.
- **Recommendation:** **C** to launch quickly, then **B** once you have 3+ products or want subscriptions.

### 2. 🟠👤 Which sea moss do you use, and where is it from?
- **What's wrong:** "Sea moss" is really two different seaweeds (cold-water Irish moss, or warm-water Caribbean "gold" sea moss). Customers increasingly ask which one, and where it's from.
- **Why it matters:** Naming the species and country (e.g. "wildcrafted Gracilaria from St. Lucia") builds trust and sets you apart from vague competitors.
- **What to do:** Tell the web team the species, the country of origin and your supplier's harvesting method. We'll add it to the product page and the Sea Moss 101 page.

### 3. 🟠👤 Can you use the word "Organic"?
- **What's wrong:** The jar label says "ORGANIC." Using that word on food generally requires USDA organic certification (small businesses have limited exemptions with strict rules).
- **Why it matters:** An unsupported "organic" claim can lead to complaints or regulatory trouble.
- **What to do:** Confirm whether your sea moss or your product is certified. If not, change the wording to "made with organic elderberry" (only if true) or remove it. The new website already avoids the word.

### 4. 🟠👤 Where is the gel made, and is it licensed?
- **What's wrong:** Sea moss gel is a perishable food. Selling it generally requires registration and inspection with the **NC Department of Agriculture (NCDA&CS)**, and home kitchens have restrictions on refrigerated products.
- **What to do:** Confirm your kitchen setup and licensing (a quick call to NCDA&CS Food & Drug Protection Division). Once confirmed, we can proudly add "Made in North Carolina" to the site.

### 5. 🟠👤 How is a refrigerated product shipped?
- **What's wrong:** The store ships with UPS, but the jar says "Keep Refrigerated." There's no shipping policy explaining how freshness is protected.
- **Why it matters:** Warm jars mean spoiled product, refunds and bad reviews.
- **What to do:** Decide on insulated packaging with ice packs, ship Monday–Wednesday only (so nothing sits in a warehouse over the weekend), consider limiting shipping zones, and publish a short shipping policy.

### 6. 🟠👤 One business email address
- **What's wrong:** The site shows two different Gmail addresses, one of which is a personal address.
- **Why it matters:** A branded address looks more professional, keeps personal email private, and lands in fewer spam folders.
- **What to do:** Set up `hello@yufemi.com` through Google Workspace (about $7/month). The new website already uses this address, so it must exist before launch.

### 7. 🟠👤 Instagram link and waitlist tool
- **What to do:** Send us your Instagram handle. Choose an email-list tool for the "Join the waitlist" button: **Klaviyo** (best for online stores), **Mailchimp**, or **Kit**. All have free starter plans.

### 8. 🟡👤 Do you have the original logo files?
- **What's wrong:** We only have a picture of the logo (from the website). For print, labels, signs and a crisp website you need the original design file (ending in .AI, .EPS, .SVG or .PDF).
- **What to do:** Ask whoever designed the logo for the "vector" files. While you're at it, ask for a version that says **YUFEMI WELLNESS** instead of "YUFEMI CONSULTING GROUP LLC" (see item 15).

## Part 2: Problems on the current website (yufemi.com today)

These are live right now and visible to customers and Google.

### 9. 🔴🛠️ The "About Us" page is about a consulting firm
- **What's wrong:** It says "At consulting firm, we are committed to being your strategic partner…" It has a misspelled heading ("Execllence") and lists James Gatabaki four times as CEO, Head of Department, Manager and Associate. This is leftover text from the website template.
- **Why it matters:** Visitors are confused, and **Google now describes Yufemi as a consulting company** in search results.
- **What to do:** Replace it with the real Yufemi story, or hide the page until it's rewritten.

### 10. 🔴🛠️ The "Contact" page talks about "consultancy services"
- **What to do:** Rewrite for customers: questions about orders, wholesale and events.

### 11. 🔴🛠️ Placeholder pages are publicly visible
- **What's wrong:** A page called "Service 1" is full of fake Latin filler text ("Lorem ipsum…") about "Business Strategy Consulting." A default "Hello world!" blog post (with a sample comment) is live, and the blog's newsletter box has filler text too.
- **What to do:** Delete these pages and the post.

### 12. 🔴🛠️ A $0 "COMING SOON" product is for sale
- **What's wrong:** The shop lists a product called "COMING SOON," priced at $0 and marked "in stock," in the Apparel and Skincare categories.
- **Why it matters:** Someone could try to "buy" it, and it makes the shop look unfinished.
- **What to do:** Remove it. Use a "Join the waitlist" message instead (the new site does this).

### 13. 🔴🛠️ The main button on the homepage doesn't work
- **What's wrong:** "Click Here to DO YUFEMI" looks like a button but is just text. Clicking does nothing.
- **What to do:** Make it a real button that goes to the product.

### 14. 🔴🛠️ The homepage doesn't show the product
- **What's wrong:** There's no product photo with a price and an "Add to cart" button on the homepage. Visitors have to dig through menus to find the one thing for sale.
- **What to do:** Put the Elderberry Ginger Sea Moss Gel, its $30 price and a Buy button right at the top. (Done in the new design.)

### 15. 🟡🛠️ "Consulting Group LLC" appears in the logo customers see
- **What's wrong:** The website logo, browser tab icon and link previews all show "YUFEMI CONSULTING GROUP LLC."
- **Why it matters:** It confuses shoppers about what the brand is.
- **What to do:** Use "YUFEMI WELLNESS" on everything customer-facing; keep the LLC name only in the legal fine print.

### 16. 🟡🛠️ Google has almost nothing useful to show
- **What's wrong:** The homepage title in Google is just "Yufemi." Pages have no search descriptions, and images have no descriptions for Google or screen-reader users.
- **What to do:** Add a clear title and short description to every page (e.g. "Elderberry Ginger Sea Moss Gel, Small-Batch | Yufemi Wellness"). Describe every image. The new site does this.

### 17. 🟡🛠️ Visitors can't zoom in on phones
- **What's wrong:** The site blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile.
- **Why it matters:** It's an accessibility problem for anyone with low vision, and an easy fix.

### 18. 🟡🛠️ An unnecessary language switcher floats over the page
- **What's wrong:** A small "English" language box appears over the content, but the site only has English.
- **What to do:** Remove the translation plugin unless a Spanish version is planned.

### 19. 🟡🛠️ The site is slow and has large empty gaps
- **What's wrong:** The homepage downloads about 2.6 MB. One product photo alone is 1.4 MB, and the logo file is ten times larger than needed. Big blank areas appear while images load.
- **What to do:** Shrink and compress images, and tidy the layout. (The new site loads a fraction of this.)

### 20. 🟢🛠️ Small polish items
- Bullet points are typed as the letter "o" instead of real bullets.
- The footer link says "Services / Products"; it should just say "Shop."
- The "Website built by The Free Website Guys 🚀" credit is more prominent than the Yufemi brand.
- The site hasn't been updated since February 2026; regular updates help search rankings.

### 21. 🟡🛠️ Remove the "92 of 102 minerals" claim
- **What's wrong:** The homepage FAQ says sea moss contains "92 of the 102 minerals the human body needs." This popular claim **isn't backed by scientific evidence**.
- **Why it matters:** Unsupported health claims can draw complaints and undermine trust with informed customers.
- **What to do:** Remove it. The new site and Sea Moss 101 page use accurate, sourced information instead.

## Part 3: Launching the new website

The new website is built and saved in GitHub. These steps put it online.

### 22. 🟠🛠️ Give Vercel access to the GitHub project
- **What's wrong:** Vercel (the hosting service) can't see the project yet.
- **What to do:** In GitHub, go to **Settings → Applications → Installed GitHub Apps → Vercel → Configure**. Under "Repository access," add **yufemi_website** and save. Then import it at vercel.com/new (choose the "Other" preset and click Deploy).

### 23. 🟠🛠️ Preview first, then switch the domain
- **What to do:** Review the free Vercel preview link (it looks like `yufemi-website.vercel.app`) with a few friends or customers. **Only connect `yufemi.com` after items 1 and 6 are done.**
- **Caution:** When changing the domain settings, change only the website records. Leave the email records (called "MX") alone so email keeps working.

### 24. 🟠🛠️ Tell Google about the change
- **What to do:** After launch, set up **Google Search Console** (free), submit the sitemap, and ask Google to re-read the homepage so it stops describing Yufemi as a consulting firm. This usually takes a few weeks to take effect.

### 25. 🟡🛠️ Cancel or downgrade old hosting carefully
- **What to do:** If you move the store off WordPress (item 1, option B or C), keep the old site running until the new store has processed real orders for 2–4 weeks. Export customer and order records before cancelling the Bluehost plan.

## Part 4: Product, safety & trust

### 26. 🟡👤 Test your sea moss for iodine and heavy metals
- **Why it matters:** Seaweed's iodine level varies hugely, and seaweed can absorb heavy metals from polluted water. Publishing a lab test ("Certificate of Analysis") is a powerful trust signal that most small competitors don't offer.
- **What to do:** Ask your supplier for test results, or send a sample to an independent food lab (often $100–300 per test). Post the results on the website.

### 27. 🟡👤 Keep health wording careful
- **What to do:** Say things like "supports," "a source of," or "people use it for." **Never** say sea moss "cures," "treats," "heals" or "prevents" anything. Keep the FDA disclaimer on every page that mentions health (the new site does). If in doubt, a one-hour review with a food or supplement attorney is money well spent.

### 28. 🟡👤 Add serving directions to the label and product page
- **What to do:** Add simple guidance like "Enjoy about 1 tablespoon a day. Keep refrigerated; use within 3–4 weeks of opening. Check with your doctor if pregnant, nursing, taking medication, or managing a thyroid condition."

### 29. 🟢👤 Reshoot the product photos
- **What's wrong:** The jar is photographed on a plain gray background, and the two "heart-shaped sea moss" photos look like stock images (confirm you have the rights to use them).
- **What to do:** Do a simple daylight shoot on a light wooden or cream surface with elderberries, sliced ginger and a spoon of gel, plus a few "real life" shots (smoothie, morning tea, desk). A local photographer half-day often costs $200–500.

## Part 5: Growth recommendations

### 30. 🟡👤 Start collecting reviews from day one
- **Why it matters:** The product has zero reviews. Competitors have thousands. Reviews are the #1 reason shoppers trust a small brand.
- **What to do:** Email every customer 10 days after delivery asking for a review (with a small thank-you, like 10% off their next jar). Ask happy friends and early customers too. Reviews must be honest; never write fake ones.

### 31. 🟡👤 Create a free Google Business Profile
- **What to do:** Sign up at business.google.com. Add photos, the product and your service area (Raleigh, Durham, Cary). This helps you show up when locals search "sea moss near me."

### 32. 🟡🛠️ List the product on Google Shopping (free)
- **What to do:** Set up Google Merchant Center so the jar appears with its photo and price in Google's Shopping results, at no cost.

### 33. 🟢👤 Offer a subscription
- **Why it matters:** A jar lasts about 3–4 weeks, so a "deliver every 4 weeks, save 10%" option turns one-time buyers into regulars.

### 34. 🟢👤 Sell in person locally
- **What to do:** Farmers markets, gyms, yoga studios, juice bars and natural grocers in the Triangle. Fresh, local sea moss is a real advantage over Amazon brands. Each market or shop is also a mention online that helps Google rank you.

### 35. 🟢🛠️ Publish helpful articles
- **What to do:** Two short guides a month answering real questions, like "How to use sea moss gel: 9 easy ways" or "How long does sea moss gel last?" The new **Sea Moss 101** page is the first one. A full list of topic ideas is in `docs/yufemi-strategy.md`.

### 36. 🟢👤 Launch the next blends
- **What to do:** The website promises blends for Beauty, Energy, Gut and Mood, plus skincare and apparel. Use the waitlist to gauge interest, and launch whichever gets the most signups first.
